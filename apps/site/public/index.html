WHAT KAIROS IS

A payment-health defence that runs for one merchant, under a mandate that merchant signed — a sealed document setting out exactly what it may do with their money, and what it may not.

A payment method is one way to pay through one bank — netbanking through HDFC, UPI through SBI, a card on one network. A busy merchant offers dozens of them, and any single one can start failing on its own while every other one looks perfectly healthy.

WHAT IT'S WORTH TO THE BUSINESS

Three separate pieces of money — and only one of them is the one every recovery tool talks about.

The loss you never take

11–50percentage points fewer failed payments, among the customers who would have landed on the broken method

The range is two different incidents: UPI through one bank failing 42% of its attempts, and netbanking through another failing 55%. The worse a method is behaving, the more there is to save.

The money you get back

59×recovered for every rupee spent trying — and that rupee already prices every customer lost to an opt-out

Counted as incremental: what came back because of the system, on top of what would have come back on its own. Most dunning numbers are gross, which makes them roughly five times larger and not a measurement.

The customers you keep

59%fewer opt-outs than a fixed reminder schedule — which sent nearly twice as many messages and still recovered less

Against that schedule: 17% more money on 46% fewer messages. An opt-out is permanent, so the channel is an asset you spend, and almost nothing prices it.

    04  HOW IT WORKS

    One incident, from the minute a payment method starts failing to the minute the ledger closes on it. Everything below happens without anybody approving anything — which is why the bounds matter more than the capabilities.

    ONE INCIDENT, MINUTE BY MINUTE
    1. 14:14Netbanking through HDFC starts failing about half its attempts. Nothing is down; it is just losing every second customer.
    2. 14:15The detector's statistic crosses its threshold and an incident opens. 93 seconds from the changepoint, which is the number the benchmark measures.
    3. 14:16The controller proposes steering. It is a proposal, not an action: only the kernel can authorise one.
    4. 14:16Terminus checks the mandate, reserves 300 paise against the campaign budget, and signs the record. The money is committed before it is spent.
    5. 14:18Checkout reorders. UPI and cards move above netbanking for customers whose payment would have landed on the failing method. A holdout is deliberately left on it.
    6. 14:41The customers it did not save in time are classified by why they failed — expired card, insufficient funds, issuer down. Only about one in eight can be retried unattended; the rest need a message.
    7. 14:52Messages compose from the library — the right words for that failure, on that method, in a language the customer reads. No model is called here.
    8. 15:30REFUSED. A customer has already had three messages in seven days. The contact cap binds and the send does not happen. The refusal is recorded with the bound that caused it.
    9. 17:02The method recovers. Steering is released and the reservation reconciles against what was actually spent.
    10. 20:30The campaign closes. Every reservation is reconciled, every action is signed, and the ledger hashes end to end.

    Two of those steps are the whole argument. At 14:16 the money was committed before it was spent, and at 15:30 something the system wanted to do simply did not happen. Scrub a real run of this →

    WHERE THE MODEL IS — AND WHERE IT IS NOT

    OFF THE PATH · ONCE · ₹8.15

    A model writes the message library: 468 validated variants across four languages, one call per situation rather than per customer. 180 calls in total. The output is committed to the repository and reviewed as a diff, like any other code.

    It also writes the operator's explanations — and refuses to publish one if any figure in it does not appear, character for character, in an audit record.

    ON THE PATH · NEVER

    At runtime, choosing what to send is a lookup, not a call. Nothing on the path where money moves waits on a provider, a quota, or a sampled token.

    A model you cannot reach at 14:14 cannot be prompt-injected, cannot rate-limit you during the incident, and cannot invent a refund amount. That is not caution about AI; it is where this particular tool is worth its latency and where it is not.

    THE PATH OF ONE ACTION
    traffic→ detect→ policy→ TERMINUS→ adapter→ ledger

    Nothing skips Terminus. The policy layer proposes; only the kernel can authorise, and it writes the record either way — an allowed action and a refusal cost the same to audit.

    03  PUT IT IN FRONT OF YOUR CHECKOUT

    Kairos is a service you run beside the gateway you already have. It never sits in the payment path: it answers one question before the page renders, reads the outcomes you already receive, and works the casualties afterwards. Everything below runs offline, with no API key, and sends nothing to anybody until you say so.

    WHAT YOU NEED

    NODE ≥ 22 · PNPM ≥ 11

    That is the whole toolchain. The benchmark is seeded and offline and the language-model adapter replays a committed recording, so nothing here needs a key to run or to test.

    A GATEWAY

    The Razorpay adapter ships, and its client and inbound webhook have both been run against the live API in test mode. Gateway and Messenger are ports — one method each, charge and send — so another gateway is an adapter rather than a fork. Nothing in the core imports a network client.

    POSTGRES — OPTIONAL

    Without it the recovery worker is a single instance holding its own queue. With it, the queue and the spend authority both live in the database and the same command is a fleet.

    RUN IT 
    $ git clone https://github.com/AmeyaBorkar/kairos
$ cd kairos && pnpm install && pnpm build

# the whole gate: lint, deps, types, 1,099 tests
$ pnpm check

# the signing key. there is no development default —
# a mandate signed with a known key is one anyone can forge
$ export KAIROS_MANDATE_SECRET=$(openssl rand -hex 32)
$ pnpm --filter @kairos/sentry start
→ listening on :8080

    Two processes, and they do not talk to each other. sentry is stateless and horizontally scalable: it ingests outcomes, runs detection, and answers the checkout. recover-worker drains the casualty queue. They share a mandate and a ledger, not a connection.

    The benchmark, the operator console and the copy library all run from the same checkout with no credentials at all — which is the point of keeping every outside system behind a port.

    1 · THE CHECKOUT ASKS WHAT TO RENDER

    One call, on the page you already have, before you render the method list. It answers in Kairos vocabulary and as the config object Razorpay Checkout takes, so a merchant on another gateway needs no translation layer — and neither reading is derived from the other, because that derivation is where a translation bug would live.

    # request
$ curl -X POST localhost:8080/plan \
    -H 'content-type: application/json' \
    -d '{"customer":"cus_9f3b2a71c4e8d012",
         "sequence":["upi","card","netbanking"]}'
    # response
{ "sequence": ["upi","card","netbanking"],
  "suppress": [], "demote": [],
  "steered": false, "arm": "treated",
  "checkout": { "display": { … } },
  "maxAgeMs": 15000,
  "reason": "nothing in force" }
    fieldwhat to do with it
    sequenceRender the methods in this order. Exhaustive over what you offered.
    suppressInstruments to leave off the page entirely — a specific bank, a specific card issuer.
    demoteMethods pushed down the list. Nothing is removed.
    checkoutThe same decision as Razorpay's config. Pass it through unchanged.
    armEcho this back on the outcome. It is how the holdout stays honest at an integration that has never heard of one.
    steeredFalse means this response changes nothing — including when Kairos failed and declined to guess.
    maxAgeMsHow long the answer keeps. Anything fresher is a request the service did not need to answer.
    reasonWhy it says what it says, in words. Always present. Log it.

    It always answers 200. A body that does not parse gets your own ordering back with the offending field named in reason — visible on the first call an integrator makes, and harmless to the customer waiting on the page. The failure mode of a payment-health tool must never be "no payments".

    Report nothing at all: point the dashboard at Kairos and the gateway delivers. Three things must be true before a delivery changes any state — the signature verifies against the raw bytes, the event is recent, and it is not one already handled. Anything failing one of them is answered 200 with the reason in the body, because a gateway retries a non-2xx for hours and a bad signature does not become good on the fourth attempt.

    What the gateway says about the failure is passed through untranslated. source, step and reason are the triple the classifier reads, and re-coding them into a private enum would put a lossy mapping between the gateway's account of what went wrong and the decision made about it.

    A payment carries email and contact. The translation takes a pseudonymiser as a required argument, so a phone number cannot reach the detector by omission — the reference above is a keyed hash and there is nowhere else the raw value goes.

    Dry-run delivery is the default and live is opt-in, because getting that backwards is unrecoverable: a message sent by mistake cannot be unsent, and a merchant who meant to observe for a week and instead messaged their customers has a problem no rollback fixes. Every decision is real; nothing leaves the process.

    With a database, two things move out of memory and they are not the same protection. Authority stops two workers double-spending. The queue's atomic lease stops them double-sending — idempotent authority hands both workers the same grant for the same casualty.

    WHAT IT DOES WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG

    Every one of these is a code path with a test, not an intention. The rule underneath all of them: a request that cannot be evaluated must not be acted on, and a checkout that cannot be advised must still render.

    whenwhat happens
    Kairos is unreachable, or slowYour checkout renders your own ordering. That is what it would have done without any of this.
    A plan takes longer than 50 msSame. The budget is measured, not hoped for, and blowing it is a fallback rather than a wait.
    A customer reference does not parseSame, with the fault named in reason. Still a 200.
    The shared store cannot be readThe contact allowance reads zero. A policy that cannot tell how much it has already spoken to somebody assumes it has spoken to them enough.
    The kill switch cannot be readCounted as engaged. Not knowing whether you have been told to stop is being told to stop.
    The audit sink failsThe admission is refused. A decision that cannot be recorded is not taken.
    A mandate does not verifyEvery action refused by name — including one whose budget was raised after signing.
    A worker dies mid-actionThe reservation expires and its authority returns to the pool; the lease expires and the next pass picks the casualty up unchanged. Nothing leaks and nothing is sent twice.
    Either kill switch is thrownEverything stops. The signed one cannot be cleared by anyone who cannot sign; the store-backed one propagates fleet-wide within one admission with no redeploy.
    WHAT IS NOT BUILT — SO YOU FIND OUT NOW

    LIVE DELIVERY

    The recovery arm needs a Razorpay account able to charge saved tokens and a DLT-registered sender. It refuses to start pretending otherwise.

    ONE AUDIT CHAIN PER WORKER

    A fleet shares its queue and its budget but not its ledger. Every record is still written and still tamper-evident; "show me everything" means interleaving N chains.

    MANDATE DISTRIBUTION

    A sealed mandate is a file somebody carries to a worker. There is no control plane that hands one out, rotates it, or revokes it short of the kill switch.

    05  MEASURED, NOT ASSERTED

    Every figure here was produced by a seeded benchmark anybody can re-run, and carries a regression band derived from an eight-seed variance study. Nothing on this page is an estimate.

    DETECTION — TIMED FROM THE CHANGEPOINT, NOT THE ALARM
    93smedian detection latency

    Measuring from the alarm rather than the changepoint would make any detector look instant. It is the single most common way this number is inflated.

    The false-alarm rate rests on two observed alarms across fourteen hours of healthy traffic, so the interval around it is wide. Read it as roughly one an evening, not as two decimal places.

    SPEND — 64 CONCURRENT WORKERS AGAINST A ₹500 BUDGET
    ₹0overspend, Terminus
    ₹100overspend, a plain budget check — 20% on ₹500, 185% on ₹60

    A budget check is if (spent + cost > budget) return. That is a rule, and rules race: two workers read the same number and both spend it. Terminus reserves before acting and reconciles against what actually happened — the software version of a locking bar that jams the conflicting lever. Not ₹524. Not "about ₹500."

    WHERE THESE NUMBERS COME FROM

    Every figure on this site was produced by a benchmark in the repository, against simulated payment traffic. None of it is from a live merchant. This is a Razorpay AI Buildathon project and it will not pretend otherwise.

    Simulated does not mean invented. The traffic is seeded, so the same seed gives the same run on any machine; the components under test are the real detector, the real kernel and the real ledger; and the harness is in the repo for anybody who wants to disagree with it.

    • Recovery — 4 hours of traffic, 300 attempts a minute, a pool of 12,000 customers, with two of those methods degraded partway through
    • Prevention — 45 minutes observed per incident, 400 attempts a minute, 20,000 customers
    • Detection — 6 failure shapes × 4 seeds, plus 12 runs where nothing is wrong at all, which is how the false-alarm rate is measured
    • Spend — the same campaign replayed at 1, 2, 8, 32 and 64 workers at once
    • Every figure carries a band from an eight-seed variance study, and CI refuses a change that moves one outside it
