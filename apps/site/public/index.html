WHAT KAIROS IS

A payment-health defence that runs for one merchant, under a mandate that merchant signed — a sealed document setting out exactly what it may do with their money, and what it may not.

A payment method is one way to pay through one bank — netbanking through HDFC, UPI through SBI, a card on one network. A busy merchant offers dozens of them, and any single one can start failing on its own while every other one looks perfectly healthy.

WHAT IT'S WORTH TO THE BUSINESS

Three separate pieces of money — and only one of them is the one every recovery tool talks about.

The loss you never take

11–50percentage points fewer failed payments, among the customers who would have landed on the broken method

The range is two different incidents: UPI through one bank failing 42% of its attempts, and netbanking through another failing 55%. The worse a method is behaving, the more there is to save.

The money you get back

65×recovered for every rupee spent trying — and that rupee already prices every customer lost to an opt-out

Counted as incremental: what came back because of the system, on top of what would have come back on its own. Most dunning numbers are gross, which makes them roughly five times larger and not a measurement.

The customers you keep

68%fewer opt-outs than a fixed reminder schedule — which sent twice as many messages and still recovered less

Against that schedule: 8% more money on 54% fewer messages. An opt-out is permanent, so the channel is an asset you spend, and almost nothing prices it.

02  HOW IT WORKS

One incident, from the minute a payment method starts failing to the minute the ledger closes on it. Everything below happens without anybody approving anything — which is why the bounds matter more than the capabilities.

ONE INCIDENT, MINUTE BY MINUTE
  1. 14:14Netbanking through HDFC starts failing about half its attempts. Nothing is down; it is just losing every second customer.
  2. 14:15The detector's statistic crosses its threshold and an incident opens. 93 seconds from the changepoint, which is the number the benchmark measures.
  3. 14:16The controller proposes steering. It is a proposal, not an action: only the kernel can authorise one.
  4. 14:16Terminus checks the mandate, reserves 300 paise against the campaign budget, and signs the record. The money is committed before it is spent.
  5. 14:18Checkout reorders. UPI and cards move above netbanking for customers whose payment would have landed on the failing method. A holdout is deliberately left on it.
  6. 14:41The customers it did not save in time are classified by why they failed — expired card, insufficient funds, issuer down. Only about one in eight can be retried unattended; the rest need a message.
  7. 14:52Messages compose from the library — the right words for that failure, on that method, in a language the customer reads. No model is called here.
  8. 15:30REFUSED. A customer has already had three messages in seven days. The contact cap binds and the send does not happen. The refusal is recorded with the bound that caused it.
  9. 17:02The method recovers. Steering is released and the reservation reconciles against what was actually spent.
  10. 20:30The campaign closes. Every reservation is reconciled, every action is signed, and the ledger hashes end to end.

Two of those steps are the whole argument. At 14:16 the money was committed before it was spent, and at 15:30 something the system wanted to do simply did not happen. Scrub a real run of this →

WHERE THE MODEL IS — AND WHERE IT IS NOT

OFF THE PATH · ONCE · ₹8.15

A model writes the message library: 468 validated variants across four languages, one call per situation rather than per customer. 180 calls in total. The output is committed to the repository and reviewed as a diff, like any other code.

It also writes the operator's explanations — and refuses to publish one if any figure in it does not appear, character for character, in an audit record.

ON THE PATH · NEVER

At runtime, choosing what to send is a lookup, not a call. Nothing on the path where money moves waits on a provider, a quota, or a sampled token.

A model you cannot reach at 14:14 cannot be prompt-injected, cannot rate-limit you during the incident, and cannot invent a refund amount. That is not caution about AI; it is where this particular tool is worth its latency and where it is not.

THE PATH OF ONE ACTION
traffic→ detect→ policy→ TERMINUS→ adapter→ ledger

Nothing skips Terminus. The policy layer proposes; only the kernel can authorise, and it writes the record either way — an allowed action and a refusal cost the same to audit.

Measuring from the alarm rather than the changepoint would make any detector look instant. It is the single most common way this number is inflated.

The false-alarm rate rests on two observed alarms across fourteen hours of healthy traffic, so the interval around it is wide. Read it as roughly one an evening, not as two decimal places.

A budget check is if (spent + cost > budget) return. That is a rule, and rules race: two workers read the same number and both spend it. Terminus reserves before acting and reconciles against what actually happened — the software version of a locking bar that jams the conflicting lever. Not ₹524. Not "about ₹500."

THE FINDING WE DIDN'T WANT
Generated copy was expected to win on writing — naming the rail, saying what to do next. Swept against the readability weight it rests on, across four seeds, it wins on readability alone: at an illegibility penalty of 1.00 the advantage falls from ₹1.05 lakh to ₹10,367, on a range that straddles zero.

The generated column does not move across the sweep at all. What is reported here is the sweep, not the headline.

WHERE THESE NUMBERS COME FROM

Every figure on this site was produced by a benchmark in the repository, against simulated payment traffic. None of it is from a live merchant. This is a Razorpay AI Buildathon project and it will not pretend otherwise.

Simulated does not mean invented. The traffic is seeded, so the same seed gives the same run on any machine; the components under test are the real detector, the real kernel and the real ledger; and the harness is in the repo for anybody who wants to disagree with it.

  • Recovery — 4 hours of traffic, 300 attempts a minute, a pool of 12,000 customers, with two of those methods degraded partway through
  • Prevention — 45 minutes observed per incident, 400 attempts a minute, 20,000 customers
  • Detection — 6 failure shapes × 4 seeds, plus 12 runs where nothing is wrong at all, which is how the false-alarm rate is measured
  • Spend — the same campaign replayed at 1, 2, 8, 32 and 64 workers at once
  • Every figure carries a band from an eight-seed variance study, and CI refuses a change that moves one outside it
